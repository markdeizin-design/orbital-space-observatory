# ORBITAL — sources and credits

ORBITAL is an independent educational exhibition. It is not affiliated with, reviewed by, or endorsed by NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI or any mission partner. Russian text is an original summary for this site; linked sources remain authoritative. No agency logo is used as the site's identity.

## Media usage

NASA imagery and model data are generally usable for educational and factual editorial purposes with source acknowledgement and without implied endorsement. Third-party credits are retained. No selected image page identifies additional copyright restrictions. ESA/STScI partner images are used for this educational exhibit with their complete credits. NASA's permission does not automatically license unrelated third-party works.

- [NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/)
- [STScI copyright and permissions](https://www.stsci.edu/copyright)

Images are locally saved JPEG derivatives for web delivery, resized without generative changes. Telescope photographs, official computer renderings, and scientific data images are explicitly distinguished in captions. Infrared and gamma-ray imagery uses assigned colors. File names and downloads are listed below. Checked 2 November 2026.

Hubble and Fermi are in low Earth orbit. Webb is on a halo orbit around Sun–Earth L2; the Earth in the opening scene is a compositional element, not a to-scale orbital diagram. Exact present-day low-orbit altitudes are deliberately omitted because orbital decay changes them.

# Earth texture sources

- `day.jpg`: NASA Blue Marble Next Generation, January, with topography and bathymetry. Original 5400 × 2700 reduced to 4096 × 2048, JPEG quality 91. NASA Earth Observatory. [Source image](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/bmng/bmng-topography-bathymetry/january/world.topo.bathy.200401.3x5400x2700.jpg). [Dataset and credit](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-topography-bathymetry/). Credit: NASA Earth Observatory; Reto Stöckli, NASA Goddard Space Flight Center; topography/bathymetry GEBCO.
- `night.jpg`: NASA Earth Observatory, Black Marble 2016, 3600 × 1800. Re-encoded as JPEG quality 90. [Source image](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/imagerecords/144000/144898/BlackMarble_2016_01deg.jpg). [Source record](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/). Credit: NASA Earth Observatory images by Joshua Stevens, using Suomi NPP VIIRS data from Miguel Román, NASA Goddard Space Flight Center.
- `clouds.jpg`: NASA Blue Marble cloud composite, 2048 × 1024, retained unchanged. [Source image](https://eoimages.gsfc.nasa.gov/images/imagerecords/57000/57747/cloud_combined_2048.jpg). Credit: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center; Reto Stöckli, enhancements Robert Simmon. This is an archival composite, not current weather.

NASA describes these Blue Marble images as freely available to educators, scientists, museums and the public: [Blue Marble source and credit](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/). [NASA image/media usage guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/). This independent educational project is not endorsed by NASA. No NASA insignia used for branding.

The scene's lighting, relative sizes, rotation speed and positioning are exhibition choices rather than a calibrated physical simulation. The ocean glint mask is derived from blue surface pixels; use a dedicated land/water mask for a closer inspection view.

ORBITAL modifications: world transforms baked; static geometry joined by material;
redundant image fallbacks removed; PBR metalness, roughness and selected material
colors adjusted to distinguish gold, silver, thermal insulation and solar panels.
